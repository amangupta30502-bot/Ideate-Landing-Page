A festival that studies the future

Since 1998, Techfest has given India's students a stage many festivals promise and few deliver: real scientists, real machines, real stakes. What began as a weekend of competitions on the IIT Bombay campus has grown into a three-day convergence, recognised by UNESCO and UNICEF and drawing students from more than fifty countries.

This year's theme, An Aetherial Renaissance, treats the festival as both workshop and observatory — a place to build with your hands and look up at the same time.

Inside the 30th edition

Six ways to spend three days on campus.

Lectures

Scientists, astronauts, and agency chiefs take the stage — past speakers have included the heads of ISRO and DRDO.

Exhibitions

A campus-wide expo of machines usually seen only in headlines: quadruped robots, race-ready EVs, cutting-edge aerospace hardware.

Competitions

International Robowars, engineering challenges, and national contests judged by practitioners, not spectators.

Workshops

Hands-on sessions in AI, robotics, and deep learning, taught by people building the field right now.

Technoholix

When the sun sets, the festival becomes a stage — EDM sets and technological shows under the Mumbai sky.

Ozone

International street artists turn the campus itself into the exhibit, live, across all three days.
